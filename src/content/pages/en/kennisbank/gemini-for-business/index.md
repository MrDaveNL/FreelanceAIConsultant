## What is Gemini?

Gemini is Google's umbrella name for its generative AI: the language models themselves, and the assistant you use to work with them. Two forms matter for Gemini for business.

- **The Gemini app.** A chat environment at gemini.google.com and in the mobile apps, comparable to ChatGPT or Claude. You ask questions, have text written or summarised, and upload files. According to Google, the Gemini app is a core Workspace service that's on by default; an admin can turn it off.
- **Gemini in Workspace apps.** The assistant in the side panel of Gmail, Docs, Sheets, Slides, Drive and Chat, plus meeting notes in Meet. Here, Gemini works directly with the mail, documents and spreadsheets you're already in.

That second part is what makes the difference compared with a standalone chat tool. You don't need to copy text into a separate window; you ask for a summary, a first draft or an analysis right inside the document. Anyone deploying Google Gemini for business usually starts with the Workspace apps, not the standalone app.

Gemini is one of several assistants worth considering. The overview of [AI tools for business](/ai-automation/ai-tools) sets the main categories side by side, and anyone also weighing up other models should read about [ChatGPT for business](/knowledge-base/chatgpt-for-business) and [Claude for business](/knowledge-base/claude-for-business).

## Gemini in Google Workspace

Since January 2025, Google has added AI features to the Business and Enterprise editions of Workspace without a separate AI licence. Which features you get does depend on your edition. As of October 2026, Google's pricing page shows this for the UK and Europe:

| Edition | Price per user per month (annual contract, exclusive of tax) | Gemini, per Google |
|---|---|---|
| Business Starter | €6.80 | Gemini in Gmail and the Gemini app; Gemini in Docs and Sheets isn't included |
| Business Standard | €13.60 | Gemini in, among others, Gmail, Docs and Meet, with broader access to models and features in the Gemini app |
| Business Plus | €21.10 | Everything in Standard, plus Vault, eDiscovery and more extensive security options |
| Enterprise | On request | Gemini across the Workspace apps, plus DLP and Enterprise data regions, among other things |

The Business plans are available, per Google, for up to 300 users. Google regularly offers new customers temporary discounts; budget on the standard price. One point worth flagging: Google also sells "Gemini Enterprise," a separate standalone AI agent platform priced per seat with its own consumption-based charges. That's a different product from Gemini bundled into Workspace editions covered here; check which one a quote refers to before comparing prices.

### What do you actually do with it?

The applications are recognisable, since they tie into work that already happens in Workspace:

- **Gmail:** summarising long email threads, drafting a reply in the tone you specify, finding what was agreed more quickly.
- **Docs:** a first version of a memo, policy document or quote text, or shortening and rewriting an existing text.
- **Sheets:** visualising data and getting a quicker read on what's in a spreadsheet.
- **Meet:** notes and summaries of a meeting, so nobody has to take minutes.
- **Drive:** asking questions about documents you already have, without opening them all first.

## Privacy and data

For most organisations, this is the part that matters most. What Google itself says in its Privacy Hub for generative AI in Workspace, as of October 2026:

- **No model training without permission.** Workspace doesn't use customer data to train models without the customer's prior permission or instruction. Your content also isn't reviewed by humans for model training outside your domain.
- **Existing protections carry over to Gemini.** The security and admin settings you already have in Workspace apply automatically to Gemini.
- **Gemini follows access rights.** According to Google, Gemini can only retrieve data the user themselves already has access to.
- **Processing within the UK or EU is possible.** Customers can restrict the processing of Gemini data in Workspace to the US or the EU. Prompts and responses fall under the data regions settings; which region options you have depends on your edition. According to Google, data regions are available from Business Standard upward; the broader Enterprise data regions sit under Enterprise Plus, among others. Note: Google's data regions don't currently offer a UK-only option, only US or EU; a UK organisation wanting data kept in the UK specifically needs to check this directly with Google before committing.

Those are the vendor's own commitments. They don't replace your own homework. Three points deserve attention:

1. **Access rights become visible.** Because Gemini can use anything a staff member is allowed to see, over-shared Drive folders surface more quickly. Clean those up before a broad rollout.
2. **Record what is and isn't allowed.** Think of customers' personal data, medical or financial information, and confidential documents. A short usage guideline works better than a long policy document.
3. **Check the GDPR side.** If you process personal data with Gemini, that belongs in your record of processing activities and potentially in a DPIA; see [AI agents and GDPR](/knowledge-base/ai-agents-and-gdpr) for the detail on automated processing, UK GDPR and EU GDPR.

## Gemini versus Copilot

Anyone using Google Workspace soon wonders whether Microsoft Copilot wouldn't be better. That question is usually the wrong one. An AI assistant is most valuable in the environment where your mail, documents and calendar already sit. If you're in Google Workspace, Gemini is the obvious choice; if you're in Microsoft 365, it's Copilot.

Where they do differ, as of October 2026:

| | Gemini in Google Workspace | Microsoft Copilot |
|---|---|---|
| Licence | Part of the Business and Enterprise editions | Copilot Chat at no extra cost with an eligible Microsoft 365 plan; the full Copilot licence is a paid add-on |
| Works with | Gmail, Docs, Sheets, Slides, Drive, Chat, Meet | Outlook, Teams, Word, Excel, PowerPoint |
| Access to company data | Only what the user is allowed to see | Only what the user is allowed to see |
| Model training | Not without the customer's permission | Prompts and responses aren't used to train the underlying models |

The biggest practical difference is in the pricing model. With Google, you pay for the AI as part of the Workspace subscription. With Microsoft, the full Copilot is a separate licence on top of Microsoft 365; as of October 2026, Microsoft Copilot Business costs €18.20 per user per month on annual billing, with a temporary €15.60 for the first year for anyone signing up between 1 July and 31 December 2026. How those licences work in detail is covered in [Microsoft Copilot for business](/knowledge-base/microsoft-copilot-for-business).

If you work with both platforms, or you're considering a switch, it becomes a genuine selection question. Look not at individual features, which change every quarter, but at where your data sits, what a switch costs, and what agreements you can make about management.

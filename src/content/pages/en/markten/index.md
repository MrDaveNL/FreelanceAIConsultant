## Why sector knowledge matters

Look at AI by industry and the differences stand out first: a hospital has different processes to a bank, a marketing team works with different data to a local authority. Even so, the underlying questions look remarkably alike. Someone has to decide which applications get priority, which vendor or build partner fits, and how you stay in control once it's live. Prioritise, choose, embed: that's the work, in every sector.

Sector knowledge matters in three places:

- **Rules.** Healthcare has medical-device regulation, financial services has its own regulators, the public sector has rules on decisions that affect citizens. The AI Act lays a further layer on top, with obligations that vary by application.
- **Data.** Health data, financial transactions and customer profiles each demand different handling. What's an ordinary dataset in one sector is a special category of personal data in another.
- **Cost of error.** A badly generated product description is annoying. A wrong credit decision or triage call is a different order of problem. How much human oversight you build in depends on that.

<aside class="scenario" aria-label="Example scenario">
<p class="scenario-label">Example scenario</p>
<p>A leasing company with 140 staff wants to use AI to process private-lease applications faster. The original plan: a model that pre-assesses every application and gives an approval recommendation.</p>
<p>The adviser tests the plan from the client's side. Advice on an individual's creditworthiness falls into the AI Act's high-risk category, with requirements from 2 December 2027. The team splits the question accordingly: AI extracts data from payslips and bank statements, the underwriter keeps making the assessment. The extraction comes from a vendor the leasing company contracts directly.</p>
<p>Outcome: across roughly 1,100 applications a month, retyping falls from 20 to 8 minutes per application, around 220 hours a month combined. An automatic approval recommendation deliberately isn't introduced, as long as there's no plan for the high-risk requirements.</p>
<p class="scenario-note">Fictional example, built from situations that come up often. The organisation and figures are illustrative.</p>
</aside>

## Sectors

For financial services and automotive, I've built a dedicated page with the key applications, the rules that come into play and the pitfalls that tend to recur.

### Fintech

Banks, insurers and fintechs work under heavy supervision. Credit scoring of individuals, and risk assessment and pricing for life and health insurance, count as high-risk under the AI Act. How to handle that responsibly is covered in [AI in fintech and financial services](/markets/fintech).

### Automotive

In the automotive chain, AI touches everything from sales and the workshop to customer contact. Many of the questions overlap with the broader manufacturing sector. More on the page about [AI in automotive](/markets/automotive).

Other sectors, including healthcare, marketing, education, aviation and the public sector, each carry their own rules and data sensitivities, and more sector pages are planned. If your sector isn't covered yet, [AI for business](/ai-for-business) is a better starting point, and what the AI Act asks per application is covered on the [AI policy](/ai-policy) page. Note that the rules for high-risk applications have been postponed: since the Digital Omnibus they apply from 2 December 2027, and for AI in products such as medical devices from 2 August 2028 ([European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/ai-omnibus-enters-force)).

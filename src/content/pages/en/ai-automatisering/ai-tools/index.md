## How do you choose an AI tool?

"What are the best AI tools?" is an understandable question, but the honest answer is that it isn't about the brand. The best AI tool for a law firm that runs entirely on Microsoft is a different one from the best tool for a marketing agency that works in Google Workspace. That's why this page compares on criteria rather than producing a ranked list.

An honest caveat first: this comparison is based on the vendors' own official product, pricing and privacy pages, as of October 2026 (re-checked against the figures first published in September 2026). I don't publish my own benchmark numbers here. And I have no affiliate, commission or partner relationships with any of the vendors named; nobody pays for a mention.

These are the criteria I go through for every choice:

- **Task:** which work needs to be faster or better? Choosing a tool before you've named the task is looking for a solution to a problem you haven't defined yet.
- **Existing software:** if you're on Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace, AI is often already in your plan. That saves an extra contract and an extra login.
- **Data and privacy:** is your input used for training, where is the data stored, and can you sign a data processing agreement?
- **Admin:** single sign-on (SSO), user management, audit logs and configurable retention periods.
- **Cost:** licence per user, any usage on top of the licence, and the time setup and training cost.
- **Exit:** can you take your conversations, files and settings with you if you switch?

These criteria match the way I approach larger selections in [AI vendor selection in eight steps](/insights/ai-vendor-selection-in-eight-steps): criteria and weighting first, names only after that.

## Best AI tools by use case

Below are the most common business use cases, with the tools that fit logically. All product facts are as of October 2026.

<aside class="scenario" aria-label="Example scenario">
<p class="scenario-label">Example scenario</p>
<p>An accountancy firm with 45 staff works entirely in Microsoft 365. Some staff use a personal AI assistant account, including with client data, for draft emails and summaries.</p>
<p>The adviser works through the criteria from the client's side: task, existing software, data, admin, cost and exit. Two options remain: Microsoft Copilot and ChatGPT Business. Twelve staff test both for four weeks on three fixed tasks, using their own anonymised examples.</p>
<p>The firm chooses Copilot Chat for everyone and a paid Copilot licence for fifteen staff who write heavily in Word and Outlook. Personal accounts are no longer allowed from that point. By the testers' own logging, they save an average of ninety minutes a week.</p>

### Text and content

For writing, summarising, translating and rewriting, the general-purpose AI assistants are the most widely used: OpenAI's ChatGPT, Anthropic's Claude, Google's Gemini and Microsoft Copilot. The differences sit less in "can it write?" and more in the environment around it.

- **ChatGPT Business** offers projects, shared GPTs, deep research and integrations with Google Workspace, Slack, GitHub and Microsoft 365, among others. How to put it to business use is covered in [ChatGPT for business](/knowledge-base/chatgpt-for-business).
- **Claude Team** offers projects, connectors and search across your organisation's own sources. More on that in [Claude for business](/knowledge-base/claude-for-business).
- **Gemini** sits inside the Google Workspace plans, in Gmail and, depending on the plan, in Docs, Meet and other apps.
- **Microsoft Copilot** (formerly Microsoft 365 Copilot) works in Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook and Teams. The different Copilot variants are covered in [Microsoft Copilot for business](/knowledge-base/microsoft-copilot-for-business).

Whichever assistant you choose, the quality of the output depends heavily on how you phrase the request, a skill often called prompt engineering.

### Customer service

In customer service, it's rarely about a standalone chat tool, but about an AI agent running inside your helpdesk. One example is Intercom Fin: according to the vendor it works inside Intercom's own platform and also on an existing helpdesk such as Salesforce, priced from $0.99 per "Fin outcome" (as of October 2026). Other helpdesk vendors offer comparable features.

The key criterion here isn't the tool, but your knowledge base. An AI agent answers based on the articles and procedures you give it. If those are outdated or incomplete, your customer gets a confident wrong answer quickly. Also set out in advance when a conversation gets handed to a staff member.

### Meeting notes

For summarising meetings you often don't need to buy anything new. Microsoft Copilot works in Teams, Gemini sits in Meet depending on your Workspace plan, and ChatGPT Business has a recording mode. Specialist meeting-notes tools exist as well.

The choice depends on where you meet, who else is listening in, and where the recordings and transcripts are kept. Because you're capturing participants' spoken contributions, UK GDPR and EU GDPR apply directly here, including what you tell participants beforehand.

### Data and analysis

For analysing spreadsheets and exports, the general-purpose assistants offer an analysis feature: ChatGPT Business has data analysis, and Copilot works inside Excel. That's strong for exploring, summarising and a first chart.

For recurring reporting, an assistant usually isn't the right place. There you want a fixed source, fixed definitions and a BI environment that shows the same number every month. An AI assistant helps you ask questions of that data, but it doesn't replace the data foundation. Why that foundation matters so much is covered in [the data foundation under AI](/insights/the-data-foundation-under-ai).

### Automation

For automating processes you end up with workflow tools. Examples include n8n, which you can self-host (Community Edition) or take as a hosted service with storage in Frankfurt, and Zapier, which connects AI to thousands of apps. With tools like these, an AI step becomes part of a process: classifying an incoming email, pulling data out of a document, preparing a draft.

Here the question shifts from "which tool?" to "which process?". How to choose a suitable process is covered under [automate business processes with AI](/ai-automation/automate-business-processes-with-ai), and how to build such a chain with checkpoints under [AI workflows](/ai-automation/ai-workflows).

## New AI tools to watch

The biggest shift in 2026 is that assistants are moving from "answering" to "carrying out tasks". A few developments I'm tracking, as of October 2026:

- **Claude Cowork.** According to Anthropic, you give it a goal and Claude works independently across your files and tools, with a built-in browser for web tasks. It's available on paid plans.
- **Copilot Cowork.** Microsoft announced on 16 June 2026 that Copilot Cowork is generally available for customers with a paid Copilot licence. It carries out longer tasks end to end across several tools. Usage is billed on top of the licence per task, according to Microsoft.
- **ChatGPT Work and workspace agents.** According to OpenAI, ChatGPT Business also includes ChatGPT Work and workspace agents, which let you run your own workflows.

Features like these are promising, but need different management than a chat window. A tool that edits files or fills in forms by itself deserves the same questions as a new colleague: what permissions does it get, who checks the work, and how do you spot a mistake? What AI agents can and cannot do is covered in [what AI agents can and cannot do](/insights/what-ai-agents-can-and-cannot-do).

My advice for any new AI tool: have one team use it on real work for a few weeks, with an agreed question the trial is meant to answer. Not everyone at once, and not based on a demo.

## AI tools for professional services firms

Accountants, lawyers, consultancies and other professional services firms work with confidential client information. That makes three criteria weigh more heavily than for other organisations:

1. **Training and storage.** Choose a plan where your input isn't used for training by default, and where you know where the data sits. ChatGPT Enterprise offers data storage in Europe (the EEA and Switzerland) as a region for new workspaces. For Claude, data on the business plans sits in the US according to Anthropic; European regions only exist for custom deployments via the cloud platforms AWS Bedrock and Google Cloud Vertex, and, according to Anthropic, soon also via Microsoft Foundry. Microsoft Copilot falls under the EU Data Boundary, with the exception of the Anthropic models Microsoft uses as a sub-processor. In Google Workspace you can set a data region (US or Europe) that also applies to Gemini; what's covered differs by edition.
2. **Retention and logs.** Can you set how long conversations are kept, and can you trace afterwards who did what? Those features usually sit in the enterprise plans, not the team plans.
3. **Professional rules.** Your professional body or regulator may set its own requirements for using AI with client data. Check your tool choice against those before rolling it out.

For services firms it's tempting to start with the tool with the nicest demo. It's better to start with the work that costs the most hours and delivers the least. Which applications pay off the most there is covered on the [AI for professional services firms](/ai-for-business) page. And make sure a short internal [AI policy](/ai-policy) is in place before rollout.

## Why not a traditional AI agency?

A traditional AI agency ultimately sells capacity: a team that builds, implements and manages. That's valuable if you already know what you want to build. But whoever earns from the build has an interest in a larger scope. That's why I've deliberately chosen a different role: I advise, help make decisions decidable, and guide execution, but I don't build or sell anything myself. My income comes from the advisory work you pay for; you can see what that work looks like in [my offering as an independent advisor](/).

That also means I'm not an AI company with developers, and I don't claim to be. Whether an agency, a Big Four firm or a freelancer is the better fit depends on your situation, not on which one I happen to be.

## My vetted partner network

For work that needs more hands, I suggest parties I know. Which party fits depends on your question. Usually it comes down to one of three types of expertise, and underneath all three sit the platforms of the major AI and tech vendors.

### Development

Partners who build AI applications, integrate them with your existing systems and bring them into production. Think of connections to language models, document processing or an internal assistant.

### Data and BI

Specialists in data quality, data models, dashboards, and the link between reporting and AI. This is often the work that needs to happen before an AI application can deliver value.

### Training and change management

Trainers and change guides who help teams actually use new ways of working. I deliver workshops and training myself. If an adoption program needs more capacity than one advisor can provide, I suggest partners.

### Platforms and technology

What a partner builds or trains almost always runs on technology from one of the major players. I know these platforms from the inside: their business plans, the terms around data, and where they fall short. These are the platforms I work with most often (as of September 2026):

| Vendor | Platforms | What it's usually about |
|---|---|---|
| Microsoft | Microsoft Copilot, Azure AI services | Organizations already working on Microsoft 365. |
| OpenAI | ChatGPT Business and Enterprise, OpenAI API | Broad use of an AI assistant and custom work through the API. |
| Anthropic | Claude, Claude API | Long documents, analysis and writing work. |
| Google | Gemini in Google Workspace, Google Cloud | Organizations working on Google Workspace. |
| Amazon Web Services | Amazon Bedrock | Access to several language models within an existing AWS environment. |
| Mistral AI and Meta | Mistral models, Llama | Open models you can host yourself, for example when data needs to stay within the EU or your own environment. |

To be clear: these are not partnerships. I hold no partner status, reseller agreement or commission arrangement with any of these companies. That means I can compare them honestly and tell you when a cheaper or simpler option is enough.

## How I select partners

Suggesting a partner is advice too, and the same rules apply to it as to the rest of my work. My approach:

- **No financial interest.** I receive no commission, fee or margin from partners, and I hold no partner status with vendors.
- **Relationships disclosed upfront.** If I know a party, you'll hear the nature and duration of that relationship before the introduction.
- **Fit the question, not the network.** First we determine whether building it yourself, buying, or partnering is the right choice. Only after that comes the question of who does it.
- **Only parties I actually know.** I don't suggest parties I have no real picture of.
- **You choose.** I suggest, you decide and contract. If you want to compare several parties side by side, we can do that with a [Vendor Review](/services/vendor-review).

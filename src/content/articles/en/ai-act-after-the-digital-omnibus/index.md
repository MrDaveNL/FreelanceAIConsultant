<h2>What is the Digital Omnibus?</h2>
<p>The AI Omnibus is not a new piece of legislation, but an amendment to the existing AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689). The European Commission proposed the amendment on 19 November 2025 to simplify the rules and delay part of the high-risk obligations. The European Parliament and the Council reached agreement in the spring of 2026, and on 24 July 2026 the amendment was published as <a href="https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/ai-omnibus-enters-force">Regulation (EU) 2026/1744</a>, entering into force on 27 July 2026. So this is no longer a proposal, it is applicable law.</p>
<p>If you don't yet have a firm grasp of the AI Act's basics, the risk categories and who plays which role, that groundwork is covered in <a href="/ai-policy/ai-act">the EU AI Act for businesses</a>. This article doesn't repeat that groundwork; it focuses specifically on what the Omnibus changes.</p>

<h2>What's being delayed</h2>
<p>The core of the Digital Omnibus is a delay to the high-risk obligations, plus a few smaller adjustments. This is the state of play as of this article's publication date, checked live against the European Commission and EUR-Lex:</p>
<div class="table">
  <div class="tr head"><b>Category</b><b>Old date</b><b>New date</b></div>
  <div class="tr"><span>High-risk, Annex III (e.g. recruitment, education, credit scoring, law enforcement)</span><span>2 August 2026</span><span>2 December 2027</span></div>
  <div class="tr"><span>High-risk, Annex I (AI in products under existing product legislation, such as machinery and toys)</span><span>2 August 2027</span><span>2 August 2028</span></div>
  <div class="tr"><span>Labelling of content from AI models already on the market before 2 August 2026</span><span>no transition period</span><span>transition period until 2 December 2026</span></div>
  <div class="tr"><span>Ban on AI that generates intimate images or child sexual abuse material without consent</span><span>did not yet exist</span><span>new, applies from 2 December 2026</span></div>
</div>
<p>On top of that, the AI literacy obligation has been softened in weight, not in date: see the next section. The registration requirement in the EU database has also been simplified, and the SME exemptions now also apply to so-called small mid-caps, though that doesn't directly affect most organisations.</p>

<h2>What still applies</h2>
<p>This is the part that often gets underreported in coverage of the Omnibus: the delay only touches the high-risk rules. The rest of the AI Act stands, and part of it has already applied for more than a year.</p>
<ul>
  <li><b>Prohibited practices:</b> since 2 February 2025, practices such as social scoring and emotion recognition in the workplace have been banned. That is unchanged, on top of the new ban from 2 December 2026 in the table above.</li>
  <li><b>AI literacy:</b> the obligation to take measures for the AI literacy of everyone who works with AI still applies from 2 February 2025. What the Omnibus changes is the nature of the obligation: from a required level to a best-efforts obligation, tailored to the knowledge, experience and context of use of the people involved. The date has not been pushed back. That's exactly why this remains a hard requirement even for AI use you don't have visibility into yet, as I describe in <a href="/insights/shadow-ai">shadow AI</a>.</li>
  <li><b>Transparency obligations:</b> a chatbot must disclose that it is an AI system, and deepfakes must be labelled as such. Those rules apply in full from 2 August 2026.</li>
  <li><b>Obligations for providers of general-purpose AI models (GPAI):</b> technical documentation and a copyright policy have applied since 2 August 2025 and were untouched by the Omnibus.</li>
</ul>
<p>In other words: the Omnibus buys time for the heaviest requirements, not for the AI Act as a whole.</p>

<h2>Deployer or provider: what does it mean for you?</h2>
<p>What the delay means in practice depends on your role. As a <b>deployer</b>, the role of most organisations that use AI, the effect is mainly breathing room on exactly one point: if you're considering or already using an Annex III application, such as a tool that ranks job applicants or scores students, you now have until 2 December 2027 instead of 2 August 2026 to meet the high-risk requirements. <!-- LINK-TODO BLOG-11-EN (HR: recruitment is high-risk): elaborate on what this means in practice for recruitment software, once that article exists --> Everything outside that scope, from AI literacy to transparency, continues to run on the existing dates.</p>
<p>As a <b>provider</b>, meaning you place an AI system on the market yourself or modify an existing system so substantially that you take on that role, the delay gives you more time for conformity assessment, technical documentation and CE marking of high-risk systems. For providers of general-purpose AI models, nothing changes: those obligations already applied from 2 August 2025 and fall outside the Omnibus.</p>
<p>If your organisation uses AI that processes personal data, your GDPR obligations keep running regardless of what the AI Act delays. Those two frameworks overlap more often than you'd think, especially with AI that prepares decisions on its own; see <a href="/knowledge-base/ai-agents-and-gdpr">AI agents and GDPR</a> for how that plays out.</p>

<h2>Why waiting is a planning mistake</h2>
<p>It's tempting to read the delay as "we still have time". Three reasons why that's a mistake:</p>
<ul>
  <li><b>The work doesn't disappear, only the deadline moves.</b> Taking stock of your AI applications, classifying each one and assigning human oversight takes just as much time as it did before the Omnibus. Only the moment you need to be ready has moved.</li>
  <li><b>Part of the rules actually started applying earlier, not later.</b> The new ban on non-consensual intimate images already applies from 2 December 2026, less than a month after this article was published. Anyone who only looks at "2027 and 2028" misses that.</li>
  <li><b>The obligations that were never delayed keep running.</b> AI literacy and transparency are not a compliance exercise for later; they fall outside the Omnibus's delay. The fact that national enforcement arrangements aren't finalised yet doesn't make them optional.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Checklist for your AI roadmap</h2>
<p>Use the delay to revisit your sequencing, not to pause the work:</p>
<ul>
  <li><b>Build or update your AI register.</b> Which applications do you have, with what data, and from which supplier? Without a register you can't fill in the rest of this list.</li>
  <li><b>Reclassify your Annex III applications against the new date.</b> An application you wanted to rush into shape before 2 August 2026 can now be planned properly towards 2 December 2027, without delaying the preparation itself.</li>
  <li><b>Check whether you already meet what wasn't delayed.</b> AI literacy, transparency and the prohibited practices are not a future obligation. An <a href="/ai-workshop/ai-literacy-training">AI literacy training</a> is a direct, demonstrable way to meet that first requirement.</li>
  <li><b>Put the new ban from 2 December 2026 on the calendar.</b> Especially relevant if you offer or use AI systems that generate or edit image content.</li>
  <li><b>Record the current timeline in your AI policy.</b> A policy that still cites the old August 2026 date is out of date as of today. How to set that up is covered under <a href="/ai-policy">AI policy</a>.</li>
  <li><b>Have the whole picture reviewed objectively if you can't assess it yourself.</b> The <a href="/services/ai-readiness-assessment">AI Readiness Assessment</a> maps the classification and compliance status of all your applications, including the current facts from this article.</li>
</ul>
<p>For ongoing oversight of that roadmap, without creating a full-time role for it, a <a href="/services/fractional-ai-lead">Fractional AI Lead</a> is the role that keeps the agenda on track and adjusts it the moment the regulation shifts again. How to organise that oversight without turning it into bureaucracy is described in <a href="/insights/ai-governance-without-bureaucracy">AI governance without bureaucracy</a>.</p>
<p>The Digital Omnibus changes the pace of the AI Act, not its direction. Organisations that use the extra time to get the basics in order will be in a far better position at the next deadline than those that wait until it's close.</p>
